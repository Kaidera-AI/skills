# Critique

Walk the user's primary task: orientation, first action, progress, completion and recovery. Judge hierarchy by what the task needs, not a generic aesthetic. In Operate surfaces, density and precision may serve expert work; on Read surfaces, headings and scan paths support comprehension; Persuade and Experience may justify more expression.

Inspect visibility of status, familiar concepts, user control and escape routes, consistency, error prevention, recognition over recall, efficient expert paths, relevant visual economy, actionable error recovery, and discoverable help. Cite concrete instances. Optional 0–4 scores mean absent/broken, major gaps, partial, good, excellent; unknown evidence is untested, while genuinely inapplicable criteria are N/A. Any total uses 4 times only the scored criteria and records the exclusions; totals from different criteria are not directly comparable.

Review cognitive load: simultaneous decisions, ambiguous labels, hidden dependencies, competing calls to action, interruptions, repeated input and need to remember information across screens. Count meaningful choices in context rather than imposing an arbitrary maximum. Consider relevant perspectives such as a first-time user, keyboard user, mobile user or an expert doing repeated work; these are analytical lenses, not observed user-test results.

Preserve successful patterns. Prioritize changes that shorten or clarify the real task. Distinguish a measured usability obstacle from a taste preference. A redesign needs its own accepted scope. Close with concrete proposed remedies and unresolved product decisions; ask only questions whose answers would change the recommendation.
